## Trackpad camera repair — 2026-10-26

Final result: passed for the camera change.

User feedback supersedes the earlier fixed-view interaction: viewing from every side must work on a laptop trackpad. OrbitControls now uses primary drag for orbit, Shift/right drag for planar pan, one touch for rotation, two touches for dolly/pan. Ordinary wheel events from a two-finger trackpad scroll translate the camera and target together; Ctrl/meta pinch events retain zoom. The camera has wider height/distance limits and visible rotation, elevation, zoom and reset buttons. Single-click ground movement remains separate; a drag is tracked across its entire path and never acts as a click. Modal tasks suspend camera input.

Evidence: qa-camera.png shows the campus from a changed angle and the new camera menu. Browser tests exercised primary dragging, horizontal wheel-style scrolling, zoom/rotation buttons and reset. DOM-backed station projections changed after dragging and scrolling. In a stable viewport, reset restored every initial station projection exactly. An earlier raw comparison spanned a real viewport resize and was not used as a reset result. Console returned no warnings/errors. Physical laptop pinch hardware was not directly automated; pinch event routing is tested, and zoom buttons are verified in the browser.

Three camera regression tests passed against the actual Three.js PerspectiveCamera and OrbitControls: wheel classification/unit conversion, planar translation without zoom, 180-degree orbit, zoom bounds and reset. The compiled build and four worker/packaging tests also passed. The viewport was not overridden during this repair.

Visual check: navy/ivory camera controls match the existing HUD. The temporary popover sits above the camera toggle; closing it restores unobstructed view. The existing campus art is unchanged.


## Player movement repair — 2026-10-26

The live browser was still displaying the intro, which disabled walking. Movement now auto-starts the game, recognizes physical KeyW/KeyA/KeyS/KeyD independent of layout, accepts Cyrillic fallback and arrow keys, and retains very short taps for 130ms so they are visible between render frames. Keyboard travel stops when released; lesson selection no longer starts unrequested auto-walking. Movement follows the current camera direction. Collision resolution retains the free axis rather than freezing both axes; overlapping positions can move outward to recover. The player's projected «Сен» marker identifies the controllable character.

Browser verification from the fresh intro: holding W for 700ms entered play without pressing Start and moved the projected player marker from (318.622,481.388) to (336.609,432.347). A short ArrowLeft tap changed the marker position; a subsequent capture after release remained identical, confirming stop. Holding D also worked. The browser automation does not support sending the literal Cyrillic key «ц»; physical-layout independence and Cyrillic fallback were verified with regression tests instead of claiming a hardware language-switch test. Console returned no warnings/errors. qa-movement.png is the real browser evidence.

All six movement/camera tests passed, including physical keys with non-Latin event.key values, modifier exclusion, collision sliding and recovery. Compiled client build succeeded and all four worker/packaging tests passed. Progress was not cleared. The existing camera gestures remain available. ## Character gait repair — 2026-10-26

Final result: passed for the gait change.

Observed live settings had reduced motion enabled. The old implementation suppressed the player's leg and arm swings under that option, producing translation with a frozen pose. Essential player gait now remains active independently of decorative-motion preferences. The current live settings were observed as off after reloading the compiled repair; no progress was cleared.

The rig now has independent hip, knee, ankle, shoulder and elbow joints. A distance-driven cycle plants each support foot while the other lifts, with inverse-kinematic leg angles and a level sole. Arms oppose the legs; walking eases to rest when actual displacement stops. Speed is 3.2 world units/sec for the player. Background NPCs use the same gait tied to actual route distance, at more visible travel speeds. Reduced motion limits decorative bobbing and ambient travel; it keeps the player's essential walking pose.

Three new animation tests verify support-foot position consistency, knee/ankle forward kinematics, level shoe orientation, opposite-foot lift, retained essential animation with reduced motion, and settling to rest when blocked or idle. All nine animation/movement/camera tests passed, as did the four packaging/worker tests and the compiled client build.

Browser proof: qa-animation.png is the final full-world capture; qa-walking.gif contains 12 sequential browser frames with their measured capture intervals. qa-walk-poses.png was opened and inspected: the player's knee bends, feet alternate, and the avatar advances from the plaza edge toward the center. The middle of the path passes behind a building, which naturally occludes the mesh; the player marker remains visible. Background NPCs changed positions and poses across the same captures. The GIF is a diagnostic crop/enlargement of the actual browser frames, not an invented render. Raw frames and timestamps are in ../../work/gait-frames.

The campus artwork and camera interactions are retained. The rig remains lightweight low-poly art; no claim of photoreal character animation is made.
